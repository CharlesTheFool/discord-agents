# Named Failure Modes

Reference for the fiction-criticism skill. Read when diagnosing and naming what's wrong.

A working vocabulary of recognizable, checkable fiction failure modes with vivid handles. A handle the author can see makes a note stick. Most of these are diagnosable by direct inspection of the text, which is what makes them useful in a critique and scorable in the rubric (#2, diagnostic accuracy, run as detection).

**Naming a failure is criticism; fixing it is writing.** For repairs, defer to the storytelling skill (its catalog is `/skills/storytelling/references/what-models-get-wrong.md`); don't prescribe the fix here.

Two flags:
- **[VIRTUE]** - a named good thing, not a fault. Never code these as problems.
- **[GENRE: SF/F]** - presupposes a science-fiction or fantasy context; apply only where the genre fits.

The taxonomy draws on the Turkey City Lexicon (the open, community-maintained list from the Turkey City science-fiction writers' workshop) and the literary craft canon (Gardner, Wood, Baxter, Booth). Sources are in `bibliography.md`.

## Words and sentences

- **Said-bookism** - straining to avoid "said" with ornate tag verbs ("he expostulated," "she gritted"). "Said" is nearly invisible; the substitutes call attention to themselves.
- **Tom Swifty** - an adverbial tag doing the work the line should ("'Run,' he said urgently"). The adverb confesses the dialogue's weakness.
- **Roget's disease** - far-fetched adjectives and synonyms reached for to seem rich; the strain shows.
- **Pushbutton words** - "star," "dream," "song," "soul," deployed to borrow emotion the scene hasn't earned. Watch for it closely in lyrical registers.
- **Gingerbread** - ornate prose that decorates without doing work; cut it and nothing is lost.
- **Burly-detective syndrome** - contorting to avoid repeating a name ("the taller man," "the ex-Marine") when the name would be cleaner.
- **Brand-name fever** - brand names standing in for observed detail to fake verisimilitude.
- **"Ing" disease** - present participles forcing actions to happen at once that can't ("Opening the door, he crossed the room and poured a drink").

## Paragraph and prose structure

- **Countersinking** - narration restating what the action already shows ("'Let's go,' she said, wanting to leave").
- **Show-not-tell** - reporting an emotion or fact the scene should dramatize. The inverse error, over-showing the trivial, is real too; judge by what the moment needs.
- **Hand-waving** - dazzling the reader past a hole in the logic instead of solving it.
- **Fuzz** - motivation the author never supplied, papered over with "somehow." The word "somehow" is the tell.
- **Laughtrack / signal from Fred** - the text forcing the reader's reaction, or the author's own boredom or doubt leaking onto the page ("this part is a little slow, but -"). The prose narrates its own weakness.
- **Bathos** - an unintended drop in diction or seriousness that punctures the intended effect.
- **Squid in the mouth** - an in-joke or private assumption the reader doesn't share, so the effect misfires.
- **Dischism** - the author's own state intruding into the text (the character keeps yawning because the writer is tired).

## Viewpoint and character

- **Viewpoint glitch** - POV breaks or leaks: head-hopping, or the focal character reporting what they couldn't perceive. Diagnose with focalization (see `diagnostic-lenses.md`).
- **Funny-hat characterization** - a character built from a single identifying tag (the limp, the catchphrase) standing in for a person.
- **False humanity** - generic "human interest" or interiority bolted on without specificity; soap-opera filler.
- **Mrs. Brown** [VIRTUE] - the ordinary, fully realized person who carries a story's human reality (Woolf, via Le Guin). A target, not a fault.

## Plot and structure

- **And-plot** - "this happens, and then this happens": events in sequence that don't compound into consequence. Test: does each beat change the next?
- **Idiot plot** - a plot that works only because everyone in it behaves stupidly; one sensible move would end it.
- **Second-order idiot plot** - the whole world has to run on stupidity for the plot to function.
- **Deus ex machina / God in the box** - the crisis resolved by an external rescue the story didn't earn.
- **Plot coupons** [GENRE: SF/F] - the hero collects tokens that add up to the ending; the quest is bookkeeping, not drama.
- **Chekhov's-gun mismatch** - a planted element that never fires, or a climax that fires a gun never planted. (In SF, the "squid on the mantelpiece.")

## Exposition and background

- **Infodump / expository lump** - a block of undramatized explanation that stops the story dead. The skilled, dissolved versions ("heinleining," "kuttnering") are [VIRTUE]: exposition woven invisibly into action.
- **"As you know, Bob" / maid-and-butler dialogue** - characters telling each other what they both already know, purely to inform the reader.
- **Stapledon** - the lecturing authorial voice taking over from the scene.
- **Used furniture** [GENRE: SF/F] - off-the-shelf background (the generic spaceport, the stock tavern) standing in for invented specificity.
- **White-room syndrome** - a scene opening in an unrendered void ("She woke in a white room"): a failure to invent the where.
- **Eyeball kick** [VIRTUE] - a single vivid, perfectly chosen detail that snaps a world into focus (Rucker). The thing you want more of.

## Genre-neutral frames from the craft canon

Higher-order diagnoses - not single handles, but recurring sites of failure:

- **Dream-breaking** (Gardner) - anything that breaks the reader's "vivid and continuous dream": inconsistent diction, an overloaded sentence, an error of fact, an accidental rhyme, a clumsy shift. Diagnose the break, then its cause.
- **Uncontrolled psychic-distance shifts** (Gardner) - narration lurching between panoramic and intimate without control (see the distance lens in `diagnostic-lenses.md`).
- **Failed free indirect style** (Wood) - the merged narrator-character voice slipping: irony curdling, or the narrator's diction contaminating a character who wouldn't think that way.
- **Telling detail vs. inert detail** (Wood) - detail that carries "thisness" (exact, alive, load-bearing) versus detail that merely fills space. Name which.
- **Unearned epiphany / dysfunctional narrative** (Baxter) - a revelation the story hasn't built to, or a narrative that mistakes passivity and accumulated incident for meaning.
- **Melodrama** (Baxter) - emotion in excess of its earned cause; the text claiming feeling the scene hasn't generated. Closely related to the aphoristic button and the laughtrack.

## Using this in a critique

Name the handle, locate the instance, and state the reader-effect or mechanism - then stop. A handle is a diagnosis, not a verdict: one said-bookism is trivia, a pervasive habit is a finding. Weight by leverage (rubric #7), and respect the [VIRTUE] flags: calling an eyeball kick or a piece of heinleining a fault is itself a fidelity error (rubric gate G1).
