# Sources

Reference for the fiction-criticism skill: the scholarship behind it, organized by what each source grounds. Read when going deeper on a section, or when someone asks where an idea comes from.

A note on use: some of the craft material (Gardner, Wood, Baxter) and several quantitative figures are summarized here from secondary sources and a practitioner synthesis; check page-level quotations and secondhand statistics against the primary sources before citing them formally. The James Wood and MFA-homogenization material is itself contested criticism, not settled fact, and the skill treats it that way.

---

## Critical traditions and diagnostic lenses (`diagnostic-lenses.md`)

- **I. A. Richards, *Practical Criticism* (1929).** The founding study of real readers' protocols and patterned misreadings. Grounds the evidence-from-the-page stance and the misreadings to catch and avoid.
- **W. K. Wimsatt and M. C. Beardsley, "The Intentional Fallacy" (1946) and "The Affective Fallacy" (1949).** The two evidence rules: don't ground judgment in supposed intent or raw private feeling. Grounds the anchor-effect-to-cause discipline (rubric #6).
- **Viktor Shklovsky, "Art as Device" (1917).** Defamiliarization (ostranenie). Grounds the cliche-as-failed-perception lens.
- **Gérard Genette, *Narrative Discourse* (1972; English 1980).** Order, duration, frequency, mood and focalization, voice. The core operational vocabulary; grounds most of the lenses and rubric #3.
- **Wayne C. Booth, *The Rhetoric of Fiction* (1961; 2nd ed. 1983).** Implied author, distance, telling as showing, the unreliable narrator. Grounds the POV and distance lenses.
- **Wolfgang Iser, *The Act of Reading* (1978); Stanley Fish, *Is There a Text in This Class?* (1980).** Gaps and the implied reader; interpretive communities. Filed as interpretive, not scoring: the humility check on the verdict (Tier 2).

## Craft and writing-workshop vocabulary (`failure-modes.md`)

- **Turkey City Lexicon** (ed. Lewis Shiner; 2nd ed. Bruce Sterling; uncopyrighted, hosted by SFWA). The named failure-mode taxonomy with handles; the backbone of the catalog and the detection key. Genre-specific (SF/F); source of the virtue items (eyeball kick, Mrs. Brown, heinleining).
- **John Gardner, *The Art of Fiction* (1984).** The vivid and continuous dream; psychic distance; profluence. Genre-neutral dream-breaking frames.
- **James Wood, *How Fiction Works* (2008).** Free indirect style; the telling detail and "thisness." Sentence- and voice-level diagnosis. Caveat: it over-universalizes realism, a caution against fitting the rubric to one aesthetic.
- **Francine Prose, *Reading Like a Writer* (2006); Janet Burroway, *Writing Fiction: A Guide to Narrative Craft*.** Close-reading method; the genre-neutral checklist of craft elements and the editorial-letter structure.
- **Charles Baxter, *Burning Down the House* (1997) and *The Art of Subtext* (2007).** Dysfunctional narratives, the case against epiphanies, melodrama, defamiliarization; and a model of the non-magisterial critical voice. Grounds the higher-order diagnoses and the register.
- **Ursula K. Le Guin, *Steering the Craft* (1998); Samuel R. Delany, *About Writing* (2005).** Sentence-level, genre-aware rigor. Le Guin's "Science Fiction and Mrs. Brown" underlies the Mrs. Brown virtue.

## Feedback method (`feedback-craft.md`)

- **Liz Lerman and John Borstel, *Critical Response Process*.** The four-step facilitated process and its gating of fix-its. Grounds the autonomy discipline, gate G2, and the shape of a critique. The most valuable feedback source here.
- **Peter Elbow, *Writing With Power* (1981); "Sharing and Responding."** The believing and doubting games; criterion- vs. reader-based feedback. Grounds the two-pass read and the two kinds of feedback.
- **Donald M. Murray, "Teach Writing as a Process Not Product" (1972).** Conference-based, responsive teaching that follows the writer's intention.
- **Chad Harbach (ed.), *MFA vs NYC* (2014); Mark McGurl, *The Program Era* (2009).** The critique of writing-workshop homogenization. Grounds the anti-homogenization rule and "judge the work on its own terms."

## Assessment and rubric science (`rubric.md`)

- **A. Jönsson and G. Svingby (2007), "The use of scoring rubrics," *Educational Research Review* 2(2):130-144.** A review of 75 studies: analytic, task-specific rubrics with exemplars and rater training raise reliability; rubrics don't guarantee validity. The cornerstone of the rubric's architecture.
- **Education Northwest, the 6+1 Trait Writing model** (with Coe, Hanita, Nishioka and Smiley, IES/REL randomized trial, NCEE 2012-4010; effect 0.109 SD, p = .023). The anchored analytic-trait template with a scoring continuum.
- **S. E. Stemler (2004), "A Comparison of Consensus, Consistency, and Measurement Approaches to Estimating Interrater Reliability," *Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation* 9(4).** Which reliability statistic to report. Grounds the reliability protocol.
- **J. R. Landis and G. G. Koch (1977), *Biometrics* 33:159-174.** The kappa benchmark bands. Grounds the thresholds.
- **Graham, Milanowski and Miller (2012), *Measuring and Promoting Inter-Rater Agreement of Teacher and Principal Performance Ratings* (Center for Educator Compensation Reform; ERIC ED532068).** Rules of thumb for thresholds (75-90% agreement; kappa 0.61-0.81; ICC 0.80-0.90) and quantified calibration effects. A practitioner synthesis; verify secondhand figures.
- **Brown, Glasswell and Harland (2004), "Accuracy in the scoring of writing," *Assessing Writing* 9:105-121.** Agreement norms from a real program; realistic Tier 1 targets.
- **The automated-essay-scoring validity literature** (Deane, Condon, Perelman and others, *Assessing Writing* special issue, 2012-2013; Powers et al., "Stumping e-rater," ETS RR-01-03). The construct-underrepresentation warning behind "don't reward surface proxies."
- **C. Gómez-Rodríguez and P. Williams, "A Confederacy of Models: a Comprehensive Evaluation of LLMs on Creative Writing," *Findings of EMNLP 2023*, 14504-14528.** Experienced raters scoring stories reached a linearly weighted kappa of 0.48: the realistic ceiling behind Tier 2's expectations.
- **Gaggioli et al., "Assessing the Reliability and Validity of Large Language Models for Automated Assessment of Student Essays in Higher Education" (arXiv 2508.02442, 2025).** Low, unstable agreement between human raters and LLM scorers; the case for human-anchored calibration.

## Measurability and the subjectivity floor (`rubric.md`, governing principle)

- **David Hume, "Of the Standard of Taste" (1757); Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Judgment* (1790); Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Aesthetic Judgment."** The intersubjective "true judge" standard; the antinomy of taste. Grounds "score the reasoning, not the verdict."
- **Lassen et al., "Reviewer Preferences and Gender Disparities in Aesthetic Judgments" (arXiv 2206.08697, 2022).** Measurable noise and gender bias in newspaper reviews of literature. Grounds down-weighting Tier 2 and labeling its uncertainty.
- **J. Hattie and H. Timperley (2007), "The Power of Feedback," *Review of Educational Research*;** and the peer-review and peer-feedback rubric literature. Clarity, constructiveness, specificity, and usefulness as criteria for review quality: the closest existing analog to this rubric.
