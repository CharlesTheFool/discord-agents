# Voice Levers

Reference for the storytelling skill. Read when setting a project's voice contract, or when a voice isn't holding and you need to say which dial slipped.

Every project has a voice contract, inherited from the bible, the author, or the works they name as references. If none exists, ask; never default to a house aesthetic. A contract stated in adjectives ("lyrical but tight") is hard to hold. State it in levers:

- **Parataxis vs. hypotaxis.** Flat coordinate clauses (speed, immediacy) vs. subordination (density, causality). Sustained either way is a drone.
- **Density.** Carver-bare to Morrison-lush.
- **Interiority.** External action only, or thought from inside. Free indirect discourse is the dial: narrator and character in one voice, irony and intimacy at once.
- **Psychic distance.** From "it was winter in the city" to "the cold in the gap between glove and sleeve." Plunge close at crisis; pull back for breath.
- **Mind style.** A POV's syntax and vocabulary should think like the character, not just report their thoughts.
- **Register and irony.** Formal to vernacular; earnest to fully ironic. Shifts carry meaning, so make them on purpose.
- **Duration.** Ellipsis, summary, scene, stretch, pause. Scene for the now, stretch for peaks, summary for bridges, pause rarely.
- **Metaphor density.** Concrete by default; abstract only where concrete breaks down.
- **Metaphor source.** Where a narrator's comparisons come from (a courier narrates in routes and ratings, a nurse in dosages and shifts). The single strongest lever for telling POVs apart.
- **Dialogue-to-narration ratio.** Shifts by scene function.

A useful contract names a setting for most levers and a reference passage that exemplifies it. When a voice already works, calibrate other voices against it rather than "improving" it.

## Testing a voice

Adjectives can't settle whether a voice works; a page can. Render a short passage aimed at one question ("does this distance survive an action scene?"). For a character's voice, a voice journal: a few hundred words of them talking, the same prompt across the ensemble, then pull the palette - sentence starters, rhetorical habits, metaphor sources, what they refuse to say, how they change by listener.
