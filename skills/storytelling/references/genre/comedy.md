# Comedy

Genre module for the storytelling skill. Read for any project meant to be funny: farce, dark comedy, ensemble comedy, satire, absurdism, tragicomedy.

Everything here describes how comedy mechanics work, not how a particular story must work. These are tools for understanding why things are funny, diagnosing why something isn't landing, and widening the options. The story's own logic, voice, and constraints come first. A comedy that breaks every rule here and serves its story beats one that follows them all mechanically.

The core insight: comedy is a structural art, not a decorative one. Individual jokes are exhaust; the engine is a system of expectations, violations, and escalation calibrated so precisely that the humor becomes inevitable.

---

## The cognitive engine

All humor works in the gap between expectation and reality. Three models describe the same event from different angles:

- **Benign violation:** humor happens when a situation reads as a violation (something is wrong) and as benign (something is okay) at the same time. A joke fails in exactly two ways: too tame (no violation) or too offensive (the violation isn't benign). The job is holding both in tension.
- **Bisociation:** a single event vibrates in two self-consistent but habitually incompatible frames of reference. The further apart the frames, the more surprising the collision.
- **Information:** the setup creates a prediction (low entropy); the punchline breaks it (an entropy spike); the listener finds a new frame that reconciles the contradiction. Comedy lives in structured surprise - pure predictability is boring, pure randomness is noise.

## Universal principles

- **Specificity is funnier than generality.** "Pontiac Aztek" is funnier than "car." Specific words are less probable (more surprising), make vivid images, signal authenticity, and reveal character. Every specific detail still has to serve the premise.
- **The rule of three.** Two items set a pattern; the third breaks it, in the final position. The "Stan Daniels turn" makes the third the exact opposite: "She's hateful, she's despicable - I'm in love." In prose scenes, think in three attempts: plausible, plausible but worse, structurally different.
- **Timing is structural.** The surprise word comes last. Timing works at larger scales too: the delayed callback, the slow-burn running gag, the convergence that pays off an hour of setup. Staying with a problem longer and resisting the first solution produces more original comedy.
- **Truth is the foundation.** Don't write something funny; write something true, then turn it funny.
- **Repetition works through several mechanisms.** The first occurrence primes, the second confirms, the third and beyond allow anticipation. Callbacks create in-group feeling. Repetition imposes mechanical regularity on living situations, which is inherently comic.

## Farce

Characters forced into credible, panicked choices that are locally rational and globally catastrophic, built with near-mechanical precision so the catastrophe feels both inevitable and startling. Farce wants seriousness, not wackiness: the seriousness is the load-bearing beam.

### Escalation

**Escalation = complication x proximity x speed.** Four phases:

1. **Spin-up:** establish fragile situations - secrets, identities, props, timers - and show why the truth can't be told yet.
2. **Cross-coupling:** each stabilization destabilizes something else. Interaction, not mere simultaneity.
3. **Compression:** tighten the constraints of space and time; the architecture becomes the throttle.
4. **Catastrophe with conservation of logic:** the only possible endpoint of an impeccable chain.

### The lie ledger

For scenes where everyone is lying, a table per character:

| Character | Truth | Belief | Goal | Lie told | Proof feared | Won't say |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Each line of dialogue is either a defensive lie or a probe that threatens someone else's. Chain reaction.

### Physical comedy in prose

The failure is false simultaneity in gerund-heavy sentences. One action, one beat: sequential sentences, staccato rhythm. Keep prop locations legible.

### Callbacks

- **Delayed recontextualization:** an innocuous beat becomes explosive seen from a different POV.
- **Repetition with degradation:** the same maneuver under stricter constraints, until it breaks.

## Ensemble comedy

### Differentiating modes

Four levers per character:

- **Status strategy:** dominate, ingratiate, deflect, martyr, threaten.
- **Cognitive distortion:** what they chronically misread.
- **Speech texture:** rhythm, verbosity, taboo threshold, metaphor habits.
- **Moral alibi:** how they justify themselves.

Test: swap two characters' dialogue. If nothing is lost, the modes need separating.

### Boss, striver, fool

- **Boss:** dysfunction masquerading as competence.
- **Striver:** escalating frustration and desperate scheming.
- **Fool:** blissfully immune to the stakes, in jarring contrast with the striver.

### Status transactions

Status is behavioral, not social, and the gap between someone's social position and the status they play is comedy gold. The see-saw principle: when one person goes up, someone else goes down.

### Attention management

- Rotate focalization so characters take turns at the center.
- Cast scenes by function: only a few drive the spine, and the rest have clear roles.
- Rotate pairings on purpose: track which pairings you've used and break them.

### Large-group reactions

A keystone reaction plus a chorus, clusters by alliance, or a sequential wavefront (see `/skills/storytelling/references/complex-structures.md`). If the reader understands the social outcome after one or two beats, it's working.

## Dark and transgressive comedy

- **Benign-violation balance.** Four dials: distance, interpretive frame, target, and tonal control. Deadpan seriousness makes extreme behavior feel mundane - "benign" - while moral debt quietly accumulates.
- **The sympathy boundary.** Sympathy is lost when there's no human reason left. Two levers: perspective as complicity (a confessional voice turns bad behavior into something human) and distance without contempt (cruelty that reveals something, not cruelty that sneers).
- **Mundane treatment of the insane.** An unbothered narrative voice: calm, precise diction, no exclamation-mark humor. The event is the punchline; authorial restraint creates the pressure.
- **Calibration.** "Too far" is the point where it stops being funny. Dark comedy needs more empathy, not less. The difference between craft and edginess: does the joke do cognitive work beyond mentioning the transgressive subject?
- **Register blending.** Earn each register on its own before combining them. Comedy makes difficult material reachable by getting under the reader's defenses. Approaches: a gradual slowing of pace, form as a benign buffer, comedy as the defense mechanism the drama eventually breaks.

## Prose micro-mechanics

- **Timing on the page:** a line break after the punch; sentence-length variation (long setup, short undercut); controlled repetition with a broken pattern.
- **The undercut:** the comic payload in an unexpected syntactic position - a subordinate clause, a one-word sentence after a build, a narratorial aside or footnote. The typographic cutaway.
- **Second-channel commentary:** footnotes, faux documents, narrator asides - a second voice with its own timing. The form promises importance and the content delivers pettiness; the aside borrows institutional styles to mock them.
- **Operative word placement:** the funny word at the end. Hard consonants are sharper than soft ones. Delay the payoff with syntactic suspense.
- **Bathos:** descent from the elevated to the mundane. Faux-formal documents are naturally bathetic.
- **Voice as engine:** the gap between what a character thinks and what they do is uniquely available to prose. First person filtered through neuroses and rationalizations; unreliable narration where the comedy is the gap between self-perception and reality.

## Drafting templates

**Farce**
- Lie ledger: truth, belief, lie, goal per character; test each beat for a reasonable choice.
- Door graph: who sees and hears whom; onstage vs. offstage information.
- Three-pass escalation: quiet, then add a plate, then force cross-coupling.

**Ensemble**
- Mode matrix: status strategy, distortion, speech texture, moral alibi.
- Pairing rotation: track and break repeated pairings.
- Choreography rehearsal: read it aloud fast, then tighten.

**Transgression**
- Benign-violation checklist: name the violation and the benign buffer for each beat.
- "Too far" pass: remove or reframe anything that stops being funny.

**Prose timing**
- Punchline whitespace: test a paragraph break after the impact.
- Second-channel build: a footnote, document, or aside when the scene needs a cutaway.
- Diagnostic: if the humor survives paraphrase, it's structural; if not, it's fragile.
